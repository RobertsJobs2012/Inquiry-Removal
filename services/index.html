Can You Dispute Hard Inquiries Yourself? Yes. Here's the Honest Truth About Why Most People Hit a Wall.

You can file hard inquiry disputes on your own. The Federal Trade Commission provides tools, and nothing stops you from writing to the bureaus directly. We believe in being transparent about that. Here's what typically happens: you send a dispute, and the bureau responds within 30 days. They either say the inquiry was "verified" — which sounds final but isn't — or they delete it. The challenge is that "verified" is not the same as "authorized." Bureaus verify that the pull happened. They do not verify that you gave permissible purpose. That legal distinction is where most self-filers give up, and where we begin. The other reality is the knowledge gap. Knowing which specific provision of the FCRA applies to a dealership's shotgun credit pull is different from knowing which provision of the FDCPA applies to a debt collector pulling your report without permissible purpose. Applying Metro 2 compliance arguments requires knowing how furnishers are required to report and what happens when they don't follow the standard. These are not things you pick up in an afternoon of research — they are the only thing we have spent 11 years doing. What you can do on your own: File a basic dispute, request your free annual credit reports, flag an inquiry as potentially unauthorized. What we do that changes the outcome: Build legally precise, individualized dispute letters that bureaus cannot dismiss as boilerplate. Apply the correct federal statute to each specific inquiry type. Follow up strategically when a bureau responds with "verified" rather than accepting it as final. Handle every communication so nothing falls through the cracks. Move as fast as the law allows because we know your goals are on a timeline. If you've already tried on your own and hit a wall, that's the most common story we hear. It's not a reflection on you — it's a reflection on how specialized this process actually is.
